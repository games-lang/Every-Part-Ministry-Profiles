Short Integrated Assessment — Content & Scoring Design

Proposal status: exploratory content design only. It is not an implementation, an approved scoring model, a validated psychometric instrument, a diagnosis, or a placement recommendation. The proposed core is deliberately 64 questions (43 integrated reflection items + 21 dedicated personality items), with a separate 15-question optional Spiritual Experience & Discernment (ESD) module.

Canonical machine-readable item-level source: reports/short-integrated-question-bank.json. That companion records every map’s category, construct, primary/secondary role, 1.0/0.5 weight, and its individual conceptual rationale. This HTML contains the review inventory, coverage arithmetic, and operational proposal; it uses no network content or application data.

1. Scope, recommendation, and honest feasibility finding

Not score-ready; content review required. Critical pruning removed indirect links rather than preserving coverage counts. The 64-question core remains a useful content prototype with 21 independent neutral personality items, but many Gifts and Strengths now lack three direct indicators. The optional experience module has one experience indicator per gift plus two unscored contextual/posture prompts; it cannot responsibly publish an extraordinary-gift result. This is an honest discovery bank, not a trustworthy automated results bank.

The 64-core count still fits the requested 55–65 range, but it does not meet the requested ordinary Gift/Strength or 6–10 APEST evidence targets after audit. Three direct answers would be a policy threshold, not psychometric proof; fewer than three direct answers is explicitly insufficient. Adding three APEST items alone would not solve the wider deficits. Do not approve a count-driven 67-core version; first conduct content review, cognitive interviews, and decide which constructs merit additional dedicated items versus leader conversation.

3. Boundaries carried from workspace memory

  • Strength prompts and results remain original, plain-language reflections and conversation starters—never a branded-test claim, diagnosis, or placement recommendation.
  • Personality remains seven flexible spectra with degree-of-tendency language, not types, calling limits, gift claims, or permanent labels.
  • APEST questions stay neutral and behavior-focused. Results may explain orientations/body pictures, but never treat them as identities.
  • The four result areas remain separate: How You Tend to Minister (APEST), How God Has Equipped You (Gifts), What You Are Naturally Good At (Strengths), and How You Tend to Operate (Personality). Serving Pattern is a synthesis, not a fifth assessment area.

7. Dedicated, independent personality core (21 items)

These are deliberately neutral paired-preference questions. They receive only the listed Personality primary map (1.0); no religious, ministry, gift, APEST, or strength behavior is used to infer personality. For every row, reverse coding is explicit: left-pole response values are 1–2 and right-pole response values 4–5; 3 is balanced. Scoring a left-pole percentage is (5 − response) / 4 × 100; a right-pole percentage is (response − 1) / 4 × 100, averaged over answered items. No reversal is applied beyond that shared polarity rule.

8. Primary/secondary mapping and cross-category rationale

A primary link (1.0) is the construct the item directly describes; a secondary link (0.5) is a narrower, defensible related signal. The companion JSON gives a rationale for every map; examples are not a substitution for that complete field-level record. Cross-category correlations are designed into this bank because one lived behavior can be relevant to several constructs. They must not be described as independent evidence between categories.

Strong direct overlaps
01–05 connect starting/building with Apostleship, direction, and organizing only where the behavior names it. 15–17 link explaining/relating faith with Evangelist and Evangelism. 21–23 link ongoing care with Shepherd and Pastoring.
Supportive overlaps
01, 02, 19, 21, 29, 31, 32, 34, 35, 36, and 40 retain a specific 0.5 link only where the same named behavior is genuinely relevant. They can never qualify a result.
Deliberate non-overlaps
12–14 are contextual only for Discernment of Spirits; they do not score that Gift or Prophet APEST. No ministry item scores Personality. 37–43 do not infer character, personality, or APEST. ESD posture items are contextual/unscored; only the single experience item has a named-Gift map.

Over-mapping audit (every item >4 maps): Q02 (5: Apostle, Apostleship, Administration, Organizing, Strategic thinking), Q19 (5: Missionary, Hospitality Gift, Relational connection, Hospitality Strength, Adaptability), Q29 (5: Teacher, Knowledge, Teaching, Teaching/explaining, Organizing), and Q31 (5: Teacher, Teaching, Teaching/explaining, Mentoring, Listening). These are all review risks; no item has more than five mappings after pruning. Broad questions never receive a long generic gift list.

Consider reducing or diversifying Discernment in cognitive testing instead of interpreting its higher count as greater validity.

15. Questions mapped to many constructs

Every core item with more than four maps is flagged in section 8: Q02, Q19, Q29, and Q31 (five each). The JSON provides a rationale for every retained score map and explicitly marks posture/contextual items unscored. Cross-category scoring may create correlated scores by design; it must never be narrated as independently confirmed evidence across APEST, Gifts, and Strengths.

16. Scoring model and evidence guardrails

For each construct independently: weighted score = Σ(response × map weight) / Σ(answered map weights). Primary = 1.0; secondary = 0.5. N/A and skips add nothing to numerator or denominator. Do not make a combined four-area numerical score.

  • Proposed publishing gate: at least three distinct answered direct/primary item IDs. Secondary maps may add context to an already-qualified score but must not satisfy the gate, inflate confidence, or publish a result by themselves. Under this audited bank, the deficits in section 13 show why it is not score-ready.
  • Three direct items is a reporting policy, not psychometric validation and not three independent proofs. Results should remain provisional and conversation-oriented after a future review.
  • Score categories independently; do not use a high Gift score to compensate for insufficient APEST/Strength/Personality evidence. Do not infer personality from ministry/religious behavior.
  • For spectra, report degree of tendency and balanced/mixed patterns, not a type. Use the polarity/reverse coding in section 7—not generic gift-style Likert means.

17. Completion-time estimate

Core: approximately 12–18 minutes is an estimate to pilot, not a proven completion time. The 21 bipolar personality items should be quick but still require reading. The 15-question ESD module adds an estimated 3–6 minutes when fully enabled. Timing, N/A rates, break-off, translation comprehension, and item reliability must be tested with representative adults before claims are made.

21. Gift ON/OFF setting behavior

Keep individual gift toggles. Disable only that item’s Gift map; keep the item if another enabled construct has a map. Examples: disabling Evangelism keeps Q15–17 for Evangelist/Strength scoring; disabling Administration keeps Q02/Q04 for their APEST/Strength maps; disabling Craftsmanship keeps Q34–36 for their remaining Strength maps. Disabling Celibacy omits Q37–39 because they have no other maps. ESD items appear only when their named ESD gift is enabled; if all ESD gifts are off, the module does not appear. Store the enabled-gift configuration snapshot with the attempt.

22. N/A and skip policy

N/A is explicit missing information, is resolved (so the participant can complete the page), and is unscored: it is excluded from numerator, denominator, evidence count, and negative-response interpretation. A skipped/unanswered item is also unscored but is a different state: it is not resolved for completion until the participant explicitly answers, chooses N/A, or uses an explicit “skip for now” action. “Skip for now” remains incomplete/pending and affects progress only as a pending response; it cannot silently become zero or N/A. Celibacy’s ineligible condition is recorded as N/A/unscored, not a negative score.

23. Save/resume, frozen attempts, and versioning proposal

At attempt start, create an immutable active-question snapshot with ordered IDs, translated item text/version, round assignment, all active maps/weights, eligibility resolution, church configuration, enabled-gifts snapshot, question-set version, scoring-model version, response anchors, and start timestamp. Auto-save every explicit answer/N/A/skip and resume at the last active position. Changes to church gifts/content affect future attempts only; an active attempt continues with its snapshot. Completion generates a derived result linked to this immutable source snapshot. This is a proposal, not an existing implementation.

24. Existing adult history and other profile components

No application, database, or configuration file was modified by this report. Existing historical adult profiles and their legacy answers/results remain unchanged; legacy flags/keys are retained for compatibility rather than rewritten. Do not reinterpret historical answers with this model. The rest of the profile remains unchanged: Heart, Story, Season, Spiritual Health, Interests, Availability, Experience, Languages, Family information, Conversation, and other existing components are outside scope.

A future version adapter may be needed to map frozen new personality/strength evidence into that existing contract; it must preserve “insufficient evidence” risk rather than fabricate missing dimensions. APEST and Gifts do not alter Serving Pattern.

25. Youth confirmation, source-overlap audit, and next decision

Youth pathways remain unchanged: children, preteens, and teens stay on their current systems. No youth content, scoring, result language, or routing is proposed here.

Overlap audit: existing source motifs retained where conceptually useful include starting new work, faith exploration, steady care, explaining ideas, practical service, prayer, welcome, discernment, organizing, and original seven-spectrum preferences. They were rewritten as shorter single-behavior items and deliberately mixed by semantic round. Closely redundant legacy variants (“people value…,” “gives me energy,” repeated encouragement/care/teaching phrasing) were collapsed. Extraordinary experience prompts were deferred to ESD; no extraordinary item is used to infer APEST or personality. Celibacy and voluntary-poverty motifs remain visible rather than disappearing, with eligibility/sensitivity safeguards.

Decision requested: do not implement scoring from this bank yet. Review all direct-evidence deficits, the four five-map items (Q02/Q19/Q29/Q31), special-experience posture boundaries, and the need for dedicated follow-through/hospitality/problem-solving/other evidence. A 67-core APEST-only addition is not a remedy for the broader bank. First choose which constructs need more items, optional modules, or leader conversation; then cognitive-test translations and behavior clarity before piloting timing, N/A/skip behavior, confidence, and dynamic filtering.

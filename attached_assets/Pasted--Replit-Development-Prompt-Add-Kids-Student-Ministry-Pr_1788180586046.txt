# Replit Development Prompt: Add Kids & Student Ministry Profiles to Every Part

I want to expand Every Part by adding age-appropriate ministry profiles for children and teenagers.

Please do not replace or significantly change the existing adult Ministry Profile. Instead, build a new age-based profile system that works alongside the existing adult profile.

## Overall Structure

Create four profile pathways based on age:

1. **Discover Profile — Ages 6–8**
2. **Explore Profile — Ages 9–12**
3. **Develop Profile — Ages 13–17**
4. **Adult Ministry Profile — Ages 18+**

The adult Ministry Profile should remain the existing profile unless changes are specifically requested later.

The purpose of the children's and student profiles is not to permanently label someone or tell them exactly what their ministry is.

The goal is to help young people:

* Discover how God has made them
* Recognize strengths and interests
* Identify people or needs they care about
* Explore ways they enjoy helping others
* Grow spiritually
* Try age-appropriate ministry opportunities
* Reflect on what they learned
* Begin developing a sense of calling over time

Use encouraging Christian language throughout.

A central idea should be:

**Every child and teenager is already an important part of the Body of Christ.**

Avoid language that suggests children are simply future church members or future volunteers.

# AGE SELECTION

When creating a new youth profile, ask for the person's birthdate or age.

Automatically direct them to the appropriate profile:

Discover

### Ages 9–12

Explore

### Ages 13–17

Develop

### Age 18+

Adult Ministry Profile

Allow an authorized church administrator to override the age recommendation if necessary.

# PROFILE 1: DISCOVER — AGES 6–8

This should be the simplest and most visual profile.

Design it so a child can complete it with a parent, guardian, or ministry leader.

Target completion time:

**5–10 minutes**

Use large buttons, icons, illustrations, simple sentences, and minimal reading.

Do not use formal personality assessments, spiritual gifts tests, APEST, or complicated scoring.

## Opening Prayer

Begin with a child-friendly prayer screen.

Suggested wording:

“God made you special and gave you ways to love Him and help other people. Before you begin, ask God to help you learn more about how He made you.”

Include a simple prayer such as:

“Jesus, thank You for making me. Help me learn how I can love You and help others. Amen.”

Add a Continue button.

## Section 1: About Me

Questions could include:

* My name
* My age
* Things I really like doing
* Something I think I am good at
* Something I would like to learn

Use visual selections whenever possible.

## Section 2: What Sounds Like Me?

Create simple choices such as:

“I like…”

* Being with lots of people
* Being with a few people
* Sometimes both

“I like…”

* Trying new things
* Knowing what is going to happen
* Both are okay

“When someone needs help, I like to…”

* Help them
* Cheer them up
* Get an adult
* Try to fix the problem

“I enjoy…”

* Making things
* Helping people
* Talking
* Listening
* Leading
* Learning
* Organizing
* Playing music
* Moving and doing things

Allow multiple selections where appropriate.

## Section 3: People & Things I Care About

Use icons or pictures.

Options could include:

* Younger children
* My friends
* People who are lonely
* People who are sick
* Older people
* People who need food or help
* People in other countries
* Animals
* My neighborhood
* God's creation
* People who don't know Jesus
* Helping at church

## Section 4: Ways I Like to Help

Choices could include:

* Welcoming people
* Praying
* Singing
* Making art
* Helping younger children
* Cleaning up
* Setting things up
* Encouraging people
* Giving
* Serving food
* Inviting friends
* Helping with simple church jobs

## Section 5: Growing With Jesus

Keep this simple and non-judgmental.

Questions:

* I like learning about Jesus
* I like praying
* I like Bible stories
* I have questions about God
* Someone who helps me follow Jesus is ______
* Something I want Jesus to help me with is ______

Do not create a spiritual maturity score.

## Section 6: Things I Would Like to Try

Let the child choose:

* I'd love to try this
* Maybe
* Not right now

Possible opportunities:

* Greeting
* Prayer
* Children's worship
* Helping with younger children
* Making cards
* Community service
* Scripture reading
* Music
* Art projects
* Helping set up
* Helping clean up

## Parent / Guardian Input

Create an optional short parent/guardian section.

Questions:

* What strengths do you see in this child?
* When do you see this child come alive?
* What kinds of serving opportunities would you be comfortable with them trying?
* Is there anything ministry leaders should know to help this child thrive?

Parent responses should be clearly identified as parent/guardian observations rather than the child's own answers.

# PROFILE 2: EXPLORE — AGES 9–12

Target completion time:

**10–15 minutes**

This profile can go deeper while remaining fun and easy to understand.

Include:

## Opening Prayer

Similar to Discover, but slightly more mature.

Emphasize:

“Ask God to help you discover the ways He has made you and how you can use them to love Him and others.”

## Section 1: About Me

Include:

* Interests
* Hobbies
* Things I enjoy learning
* Things I think I do well
* Things other people say I do well
* Something I would like to become better at

## Section 2: How I Tend to Operate

This should connect with the original Every Part personality framework but must NOT copy Myers-Briggs terminology or scoring.

Explore tendencies such as:

### People Energy

* I get energy from being around lots of people
* I usually prefer smaller groups
* It depends

### Thinking Out Loud

* I often talk while I figure things out
* I usually think before I talk
* It depends

### Planning

* I like having a plan
* I like figuring things out as I go
* I enjoy both

### Decisions

* I often notice how a decision affects people
* I often notice what makes the most sense
* I think about both

### Action

* I like jumping in and doing something
* I like watching and understanding first
* It depends

Do not assign four-letter personality types.

Create a simple descriptive summary instead.

Example:

“You often enjoy working with a smaller group, thinking before speaking, and knowing what the plan is. You also care about how decisions affect people.”

# PROFILE 3: DEVELOP — AGES 13–17

Target completion time:

**15–25 minutes**

This should feel more mature and begin transitioning toward the adult Ministry Profile.

Include:

## Opening Prayer & Calling

Begin with prayer and a brief explanation that God calls every Christian to participate in His mission.

Avoid implying that calling only means becoming a pastor.

## Section 1: About Me

Include:

* Interests
* Skills
* Hobbies
* School activities
* Work experience if applicable
* Things others often ask me to help with
* Things I would like to learn
* Leadership experiences

## Section 2: How I Tend to Operate

Use the Every Part original personality framework.

Explore:

* People energy
* Processing style
* Planning/flexibility
* People/logic considerations
* Action/reflection
* Leadership/support tendencies
* Conflict tendencies
* Team preferences

Provide descriptive results rather than a personality type code.

## Section 3: Spiritual Gifts Exploration

Explain that a profile can help identify possible gifts but cannot definitively tell someone what gifts God has given them.

Explore gifts such as:

* Giving
* Helps
* Hospitality
* Leadership
* Mercy
* Shepherding
* Teaching
* Wisdom
* Service
* Discernment

Use age-appropriate questions and examples.

**“Gifts you may want to explore further.”**

# RESULTS PHILOSOPHY

This is very important.

Do not make children's or teenagers' results sound permanent.

Avoid statements like:

“You are a leader.”

Instead use:

“You appear to enjoy taking initiative and helping a group move forward.”

Avoid:

“Your spiritual gift is teaching.”

Instead use:

“Teaching is one of the gifts you may want to explore further.”

Include this message somewhere in every youth result:

**You are still growing and discovering how God has made you. These results are a starting point for conversations, prayer, serving, and learning—not a permanent label.**

---

# PROFILE SUMMARY FOR CHURCH LEADERS

Authorized church leaders should be able to view an age-appropriate summary.

For example:

**Name:** Jordan
**Profile:** Explore
**Age:** 10

**Enjoys:** Art, music, younger children

**How Jordan tends to operate:** Thoughtful, prefers smaller groups, enjoys knowing the plan, cares strongly about people.

**People & needs Jordan cares about:** Younger children, lonely people, animals.

**Ways Jordan enjoys helping:** Encouragement, creativity, helping younger children.

**Would like to try:** Kids worship, encouragement cards, community service.

**Faith-growth interest:** Learning how to pray for others.

**Parent observation:** Patient with younger children and loves making things for people.

Make the summary easy for a pastor, children's director, youth pastor, or ministry leader to scan.

---

# DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

Keep all three youth profiles consistent with the existing Every Part branding.

However, make the children's profiles feel warmer, more visual, and more interactive.

Use:

* Progress indicators
* Large buttons
* Cards
* Icons
* Friendly illustrations where appropriate
* Short sections
* Save-and-return functionality
* Mobile-first design

For younger children especially, avoid large amounts of text on one screen.

---

# SAFETY & PERMISSIONS

Because these are profiles for minors:

* Do not publicly display children's profiles.
* Only authorized church leaders and appropriate parent/guardian accounts should have access.
* Do not allow general church members to search children's profiles.
* Keep parent/guardian observations separate from the child's answers.
* Do not use children's information for unrelated purposes.
* Build the system so churches can control who can view youth profiles.
* Avoid unnecessary collection of sensitive information.

Do not make assumptions about legal compliance requirements. Structure the system so privacy and parental-consent controls can be expanded later.

---

# FUTURE FEATURE: PROFILE GROWTH

Design the database so the same person can complete different profiles as they grow.

For example:

Age 7 — Discover
Age 10 — Explore
Age 14 — Develop
Age 18 — Adult Ministry Profile

Do not overwrite previous profiles.

Preserve previous completed profiles so development can eventually be viewed over time.

Eventually I want Every Part to be able to show:

**How a person's strengths, interests, ministry experiences, and sense of calling have developed over time.**

Do not build complex longitudinal analytics yet, but make sure the database structure will support this later.

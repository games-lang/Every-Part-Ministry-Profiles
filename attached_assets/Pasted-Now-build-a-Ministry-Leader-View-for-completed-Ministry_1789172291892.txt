Now build a “Ministry Leader View” for completed Ministry Profiles.

This view is NOT visible to the participant unless church permissions allow it.

The purpose is to help pastors and ministry leaders prepare for a meaningful ministry conversation.

The goal is NOT:
“Here are the people you can use.”

The goal is:
“Here is what we currently understand about this person so you can listen, pray, ask better questions, and help them discover their part.”

--------------------------------
LEADER SNAPSHOT
--------------------------------

At the top show:

Name
Age/life stage when appropriate
Congregation/service
Languages
Profile completion date

APEST
APEST body picture

Primary Ministry Tendency
Ministry tendency body picture

Top spiritual gifts
Top passions
Primary ministry theme

Current availability

Previous ministry experience

--------------------------------
MINISTRY FIT
--------------------------------

Create suggested areas in three categories:

STRONG ALIGNMENT
WORTH EXPLORING
POSSIBLE STRETCH / DEVELOPMENT

Every recommendation must include WHY.

Example:

Discipleship / Mentoring
Strong Alignment

Signals:
- Shepherd APEST
- Mercy gift
- Teaching gift
- strong interest in spiritual growth
- relational ministry preference
- previous small-group experience

Do NOT create a mysterious AI recommendation.

Allow the leader to click “Why?” and see which profile signals contributed to the recommendation.

--------------------------------
IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
--------------------------------

Separate:

ABILITY
INTEREST
CALLING SIGNALS
AVAILABILITY
EXPERIENCE

For example, someone may be:
Highly capable
Highly interested
But unavailable

That should NOT produce a strong immediate ministry-placement recommendation.

Display something such as:

“Strong potential fit — current availability may make this a future rather than immediate opportunity.”

--------------------------------
PASTORAL CONSIDERATIONS
--------------------------------

Create a section called:

“Things Worth Discussing”

This can identify things such as:

- limited time
- family responsibilities
- possible overcommitment
- very broad ministry interests
- limited experience
- leadership potential
- cross-cultural potential
- desire for training
- uncertainty about calling
- strengths that may be underused
- areas where answers seem inconsistent and are worth discussing

Do not diagnose, judge, or label the person negatively.

Use language such as:

“This may be worth discussing…”

--------------------------------
CONVERSATION GUIDE
--------------------------------

Generate 5–8 personalized questions for the pastor.

Examples:

“You indicated that watching others grow is especially meaningful to you. Can you tell me about a time you experienced that?”

“You appear interested in several ministry areas. Which one gives you the most energy when you imagine serving there?”

“You have strong organizational tendencies but also indicated that family time is important right now. What level of responsibility would feel healthy in this season?”

“Your profile suggests you connect across several of our congregations. Do you enjoy serving as a bridge between different groups?”

Make these specific to the profile.

--------------------------------
PASTOR NOTES
--------------------------------

Allow pastors to add private notes after the conversation.

Include fields such as:

What I heard
What seems to bring this person life
Areas to explore
Areas to avoid for now
Training/development needed
Next step
Follow-up date

These notes should NEVER alter the person's original assessment answers.

--------------------------------
MINISTRY MATCHING
--------------------------------

Prepare this data structure so that Part Finder can later use it.

Part Finder should eventually be able to answer questions such as:

“Who might be worth talking to about helping lead a teen discipleship group?”

But Part Finder should recommend people FOR A CONVERSATION, not assign them.

Language should be:

“These people may be worth prayerfully talking with.”

Never:

“These are the best people for the job.”

--------------------------------
PRIVACY
--------------------------------

Make sure churches can control who can access Leader View.

Sensitive personal information should not automatically be visible to every ministry team leader.

Build the permissions system so future roles could include:

Church Admin
Pastor
Ministry Leader
Participant

Do not expose private pastoral notes to participants or unauthorized leaders.

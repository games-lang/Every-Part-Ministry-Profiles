Create a section in the Ministry Profile called:

# Personality

Use this subtitle:

**How You Tend to Operate**

The purpose of this section is to help people understand how they naturally tend to interact, process, decide, plan, work with others, and approach responsibility.

This should be an original, ministry-focused personality assessment. Do not copy Myers-Briggs questions, terminology, descriptions, scoring, branding, or copyrighted content.

Use this introduction:

“God has created each person with a unique personality. This section is designed to help you understand how you naturally tend to operate — how you gain energy, make decisions, approach plans, process information, work with others, and respond to different environments.

There are no good or bad personality results. Every style brings strengths to the Body of Christ, and God can also grow and stretch us beyond what feels most natural.”

Add this note:

“Your personality does not determine your calling, spiritual gifts, or value. It is simply one part of understanding how you may naturally approach life and ministry.”

## Personality Dimensions

Measure these seven areas:

**1. Social Energy — Reflective ↔ Interactive**

How someone tends to gain and spend social energy.

Reflective people may enjoy smaller groups, deeper conversations, quiet processing, and time alone to recharge.

Interactive people may gain energy through conversation, activity, group interaction, and being around others.

**2. Decision Lens — Relational ↔ Principled**

What someone tends to consider first when making decisions.

Relational people may naturally consider people, relationships, compassion, emotional impact, and how others will be affected.

Principled people may naturally consider logic, consistency, fairness, standards, facts, and what solution makes the most sense.

**3. Planning Style — Adaptive ↔ Settled**

How someone tends to approach plans and decisions.

Adaptive people may enjoy flexibility, keeping options open, adjusting as they go, and responding to changing circumstances.

Settled people may prefer clear expectations, schedules, deadlines, decisions, and knowing what comes next.

**4. Focus Style — Detail ↔ Big Picture**

What someone naturally tends to notice.

Detail-oriented people may notice practical needs, specific information, logistics, steps, and what needs attention right now.

Big-picture people may notice patterns, possibilities, future direction, ideas, connections, and what something could become.

**5. Action Style — Support ↔ Initiate**

How someone tends to respond when work needs to be done.

Support-oriented people may naturally strengthen, improve, assist, maintain, and help existing efforts succeed.

Initiate-oriented people may naturally start things, suggest new approaches, create momentum, and move ideas into action.

**6. Pace Preference — Steady ↔ Dynamic**

What type of pace tends to feel most natural.

Steady people may thrive with consistency, predictable rhythms, focused responsibilities, and sustainable routines.

Dynamic people may enjoy variety, change, multiple responsibilities, urgency, and fast-moving environments.

**7. Work Style — Independent ↔ Collaborative**

How someone tends to work most naturally.

Independent people may enjoy autonomy, focused responsibility, personal ownership, and being trusted to complete a task.

Collaborative people may enjoy shared responsibility, brainstorming, interaction, feedback, and accomplishing things together.

## Results

At the end, title the results:

# How You Tend to Operate

Show each of the seven personality dimensions as a spectrum.

For example:

**Social Energy**
Reflective — 72%
Interactive — 28%

Then provide a short explanation:

“You tend to process internally and may recharge through quieter environments, deeper conversations, or time alone.”

Do the same for every dimension.

Do not place people into rigid personality types.

Instead, show tendencies and percentages.

Use language such as:

* Slight tendency
* Moderate tendency
* Strong tendency
* Very strong tendency
* Balanced

The results should help the person understand themselves without implying that they are permanently limited to one way of behaving.

## Overall Personality Summary

After the seven dimensions, create a section called:

### Your Personality at a Glance

Generate a short personalized summary combining the person's strongest tendencies.

For example:

“You tend to operate as a thoughtful, structured, and relationship-aware person. You often prefer time to process before responding, appreciate clear expectations, and naturally consider how decisions affect people. You may work best when responsibilities are clear and you have the opportunity to build meaningful relationships while following through on important details.”

Keep the language positive, balanced, and easy to understand.

## Ministry Connection

End with a short section called:

### What This May Mean in Ministry

Explain how the person's personality tendencies may influence:

* How they interact with people
* How they communicate
* How they respond to change
* How they make decisions
* How much structure they prefer
* How they work on a team
* How they handle responsibility
* What types of ministry environments may naturally energize them

Do not use personality results to tell someone what ministry they are or are not called to.

Use this closing statement:

“Personality helps describe how you tend to operate, not what God can or cannot call you to do. God often uses both our natural strengths and the areas where He is stretching us.”
